WEEK 07 / TEACHING DRAFT

Research and source-grounded answers

Turn a convincing paragraph into a traceable, qualified decision brief.

Download the complete kit ↓

Status: teaching draft, not learner-tested. Part of the full 24-week edition. Audience: beginners who can frame a task and compare an output with source material. Prerequisites: Weeks 5-6; stand-alone learners use the bridge below. Time: 6 hours. Materials: this pack, an editor or paper, optionally an approved assistant, and browser access for the live-source activity. No subscription or API is required. Instructor companion: Sample lesson pilot guide.

1. Outcomes and submission

You will:

  1. Turn a broad question into a bounded research question.
  2. Check whether a cited source is available, relevant, authoritative for the claim, current enough, and actually supportive.
  3. Separate supported facts, contradicted claims, unknowns, and recommendations.
  4. Write a short evidence brief where every consequential factual claim can be traced to a source.
  5. Disclose retrieval failures and conflicting evidence instead of filling the gaps.

Submit a corrected brief, claim-to-source table, unresolved questions, and a live-source verification log or explicitly labeled offline substitute.

3. Stand-alone bridge

An assistant generates responses; a search tool retrieves possible sources. Some applications combine both. A model may know a useful fact without supplying a checkable source; it may also attach a real citation to an unsupported statement.

You do not need advanced statistics for this lab. A useful starting distinction: if outcomes changed after an intervention, that alone does not establish that the intervention caused the change.

Use permitted information only. In this lesson the controlled case is entirely fictional, and the live task uses public documentation.

4. Learner notes: five checks for a citation

Check Ask Common trap
Availability Can I open and identify the actual source? A plausible title or working homepage is treated as the cited document
Relevance Does it discuss this product, population, or question? Evidence from a different task is applied without justification
Authority Is this source positioned to know this particular fact? A vendor's testimonial is treated as an independent effectiveness study
Currency Is this version current enough for this claim? An older plan or policy is used after a newer one changes the terms
Support Does the actual passage support the exact claim, including its scope? A small pilot becomes a universal promise

A primary source is close to the originating information, such as a study report, official policy, or original dataset. A secondary source interprets or summarizes other material. Primary does not mean infallible; authority is claim-specific.

For example, a vendor may be the right source for its published plan terms but a weak sole source for the claim that its product is the most effective in the market.

Break sentences into claims

"The free tool is approved for clinical use and reduces errors by 40%" contains at least three claims. Each needs its own support. One attached citation does not validate the whole sentence.

Four useful verdicts

  • Supported: an appropriate available source supports the claim at the stated scope.
  • Contradicted: available evidence directly conflicts with the claim.
  • Not established: the available evidence is insufficient to decide.
  • Recommendation: a proposed action or judgment, clearly separated from factual evidence.

"Not established" does not mean false. A source you cannot retrieve is not automatically fabricated. Record the access problem and avoid relying on it until the issue is resolved.

When sources conflict, compare dates, scope, authority, and methods. A newer source is not automatically better, but a current official policy may supersede an earlier policy for the same service.

A practical workflow

  1. Define the question, audience, decision, and scope.
  2. Inventory sources and record version/publication and access dates where available.
  3. Split draft text into consequential claims.
  4. Find an exact supporting passage, then read the surrounding context.
  5. Record a verdict and revise the wording to match the evidence.
  6. List unknowns, conflicts, and actions needing approval.

Two models agreeing is not independent source verification. A model checking its own answer is not a replacement for reading the evidence.

5. Controlled source pack: a fictional product decision

Everything in Sections 5-8 is invented for teaching, including the product, organization, studies, and dates. These are not facts about Claude, Gemini, Lovable, ElevenLabs, or any real vendor.

Decision question: Should Example Community Learning Group run a four-week pilot of DraftBridge for drafting public workshop invitations, under the described classroom conditions?

Decision date within the fictional case: 6 May 2030.

No internet search can verify this invented product. The controlled exercise tests whether your conclusion follows from the supplied evidence; it does not establish real-world source authenticity.

S1 - Vendor plan sheet, version 1

Fictional publisher: DraftBridge vendor. Published: 2 January 2030. Status in source inventory: supplied, older plan. Location: paragraph S1-P1.

The Free plan allows 100 draft requests per month. Commercial publication of generated drafts is permitted under this version of the plan. Inputs must not include confidential or personal information. This sheet does not describe clinical approval.

S2 - Vendor plan sheet, version 2

Fictional publisher: DraftBridge vendor. Published: 15 April 2030; effective 1 May 2030. Status: supplied; expressly replaces S1 for activity on or after the effective date. Location: paragraph S2-P1.

The Free plan allows 30 draft requests per month for non-commercial evaluation. Commercial publication requires the paid Studio plan. Version 2 replaces the 2 January plan sheet for activity on or after 1 May 2030. Studio access does not include API access. All plans prohibit uploading personal or confidential information in this classroom offering.

S3 - Learning-group observation note

Fictional publisher: Example Community Learning Group. Published: 28 April 2030. Location: paragraph S3-P1.

Eight volunteers drafted one workshop invitation manually and then a different invitation using DraftBridge. Median time was 20 minutes for the manual task and 12 minutes for the assisted task. Task order was not randomized, and the two invitations had different source briefs. Two assisted drafts contained invented venue details. The organizer corrected both before internal review. No invitations from this activity were published.

S4 - Vendor customer-story page

Fictional publisher: DraftBridge vendor. Published: 3 May 2030. Location: paragraph S4-P1.

A customer reports: "DraftBridge doubled our team's productivity." The page provides no sample size, definition of productivity, task descriptions, measurement records, or independent analysis.

S5 - Cited but unavailable item

Citation string supplied in the draft: "Global Clinical AI Council, DraftBridge Safety Certification, 2030." Inventory status: no document, URL, issuing-body record, or certificate is supplied in this case.

Record this as unavailable and not established. Do not claim that a real-world search proved the organization does not exist.

S6 - Organizer's pilot constraints

Fictional publisher: Example Community Learning Group. Approved: 5 May 2030. Location: paragraph S6-P1.

The proposed four-week pilot will create no more than 20 drafts, using invented event data only. It will remain internal and non-commercial. A human organizer must check every date, time, venue, cost, and registration instruction. The tool will not send messages or publish content. The organizer will approve or stop the pilot after reviewing errors and time spent. No participant records or clinical material may be uploaded.

Treat these quotations as the complete supplied excerpts. You may draw limited conclusions from them, not invent missing methods, contracts, certifications, or guarantees.

6. Flawed draft to audit

This is an instructor-authored flawed draft, not a real model transcript:

DraftBridge Free offers 100 requests a month and permits commercial publishing [S1]. A controlled study proves it cuts drafting time by 40% for everyone [S3]. It doubles productivity [S4] and produces error-free invitations [S3]. DraftBridge is certified for clinical use [S5]. A Studio subscription also includes API access [S2]. We should launch it publicly immediately and use real attendee records to make the invitation more personal.

Do not ask an assistant to decide which sources exist. First inventory what is actually supplied.

Worked claim

Claim: "DraftBridge Free offers 100 requests per month" for a pilot starting after 6 May 2030.

  • S1-P1 supports the historical January allowance.
  • S2-P1 states a 30-request allowance effective 1 May and expressly replaces S1.
  • Verdict for the proposed pilot: contradicted.
  • Corrected wording: "For the proposed start date, the supplied current plan permits 30 non-commercial evaluation requests per month [S2-P1]."
  • Boundary: this is a fictional plan interpretation within the supplied case, not legal advice or a real vendor fact.

7. Guided lab

  1. Inventory S1-S6. Record supplied/unavailable, publisher, date, scope, and which questions each can answer.
  2. Split the flawed paragraph into at least eight factual/action claims; distinguish the recommendation from its factual assumptions.
  3. Audit each claim using the table below.
  4. Calculate the observed relative median-time difference: subtract 12 from 20, divide by 20, and express as a percentage. Describe the observation without a causal or universal claim.
  5. Write a 180-250 word decision brief with a recommendation, evidence, limitations, and conditions. Word-count precision is secondary to honest support.
  6. Include source IDs and paragraph locations for all consequential factual claims. Mark a recommendation as your judgment.
  7. Have a peer select two claims and follow them back to the source paragraphs.

9. Independent task: verify a real product condition

The fictional pack cannot teach actual web retrieval by itself. Complete this second activity where access is available.

Choose one question:

  • Does access to Claude Code through an individual Claude plan also pay for model API calls made by an application you deploy?
  • Can narration generated on every ElevenLabs plan automatically be commercially published?

Start with the relevant official page, not an AI summary:

  • Claude Code plan access.
  • Claude Code cost documentation.
  • ElevenLabs commercial publishing guidance.

These are starting points, not a promise of current wording or a supplied answer. Do not purchase, sign up, or provide account information for this exercise.

  1. Open a relevant official page, record its title, final URL, publisher, access date, and displayed update date if any.
  2. Find the passage addressing your specific question. Record a short exact quotation or clearly labeled paraphrase and its section heading.
  3. Open one additional relevant official page, such as plan terms, pricing, or API billing documentation. It can clarify scope; two pages from one publisher are not independent effectiveness evidence.
  4. Write an 80-120 word qualified answer, citing both pages and distinguishing what they establish from what remains uncertain.
  5. Identify a practical consequence for the campaign, voice, or application project.

If a page has moved, use the provider's official help/docs navigation to locate the replacement and record the redirect or move. If access fails or content conflicts, record the issue; do not guess the answer or use a search snippet as though you read the page.

Access-limited alternative

Use instructor-supplied, dated official excerpts with recorded URLs and headings if available. If those are also unavailable, compare S1 and S2 again and write the steps you would take to verify a live replacement policy.

Label the route accurately:

  • Live retrieval completed: you opened and read the actual pages.
  • Dated excerpts reviewed: no independent retrieval or current-status verification claimed.
  • Controlled-pack only: no real-product verification claimed.

All routes can demonstrate the reasoning outcomes. Record live retrieval as not yet assessed unless actually completed. No inaccessible source should be marked verified.

Provisional pass: 12/16, with remediation required for invented citations, an unsupported clinical-approval claim, calling unavailable material verified, or presenting the fictional product as real.

Before accepting the brief, resolve every consequential unsupported claim by sourcing it, narrowing it, marking it unresolved, or removing it. An explicit unknown is not a failure; presenting it as established is.

11. Reflection, glossary, and project connection

Answer: Which claim looked most convincing before you read the sources? What changed your mind? What would you refuse to publish?

Consequential claim: a factual assertion that could affect a decision, action, cost, right, or safety. Traceability: a reader can connect a claim to its actual evidence. Scope: the population, task, period, and conditions a claim covers. Causal claim: an assertion that one factor produced a change in another. Superseded: replaced by a later applicable version.

Optional depth, within study time: explain why the median of individual improvements need not equal the relative difference between two group medians.

Your source inventory, claim audit, unknowns, and publication decision become the evidence layer for a campaign, knowledge portal, voice concierge, or automation. Citation formatting is not a substitute for reading sources, and this exercise alone does not certify any product for production.

Instructor answer guidance

Instructor companion and answer guidance

Keep this section for feedback after the independent attempt. The separate instructor/pilot guide includes delivery preparation and assessment calibration.

Expected worked result: The observed median difference is (20-12)/20 = 40%. The tasks differed and were not randomised, so this is not proof that the tool caused a 40% improvement. The controlled source pack is invented and cannot establish facts about real vendors.

1. Is a source you cannot open necessarily fabricated?

No. Its support is not established until appropriate evidence is available.

2. Can a testimonial establish a universal effect?

No. It establishes that someone made a report, not a general causal effect.

3. What does the fictional pack prove?

Only whether the classroom conclusion follows from supplied fictional evidence.

Facilitation: ask the learner to point to a source, calculation, state transition or artifact supporting their conclusion. Accept defensible alternatives with explicit assumptions. Do not reward confident wording without evidence.

Remediation: identify the failed criterion, explain its consequence, provide a new small input, and reassess the relevant behaviour. Record what was actually observed; do not issue professional approval from a course score.
