WEEK 20 / TEACHING DRAFT

Professional handover

Make the workflow usable by someone who did not build it.

Download the complete kit ↓

Status: teaching draft, not learner-tested. Fictional data and worked examples are labelled throughout. No production service is deployed by completing this lesson.

Time: 6 hours (360 learning minutes), with breaks additional. Prerequisites: the earlier course phases; review unfamiliar terms with the instructor.

Materials: this guide, the supplied workbook and diagram, a document editor or paper, and optionally an approved AI assistant. Do not buy a subscription for a lab without checking the dated cohort access plan.

Learning outcomes

  • Write an operating procedure with stop conditions.
  • Transfer ownership without exposing credentials.
  • Observe a peer operating the workflow independently.

Six-hour plan

Start with a prediction

Before reading the worked solution, write what you expect to happen, what could be wrong, and what evidence you would need to trust the result. Keep the note for your final reflection.

Learner explanations

A handover is a test

If another person cannot operate the workflow from its documentation, an important part is unfinished. State who it is for, what inputs are permitted, what good output looks like, and when to stop. Screenshots may help, but should not be the only instructions.

Operate and maintain

Name a source owner, technical/service owner and professional approver where those roles differ. Record account ownership, renewals, limits, monitoring and update frequency. Transfer credentials using an approved secret-sharing process, not in the learner document.

Prototype is a valid status

A studio handover can be for a restricted pilot. State whether controls are simulated or implemented and whether actual publication occurred. The same project can continue into the capstone with a justified independent extension and fresh evaluation plan.

Glossary

Term Plain-language meaning
Runbook Practical instructions for operating, correcting and stopping a workflow.
Owner The named role accountable for maintenance, cost and decisions.
Fallback An explicit alternative when the normal route fails.
Handover Transfer of usable artifacts, responsibilities and operating knowledge.

Worked example

During a peer walkthrough, the reviewer discovers the registration field is absent. The runbook should direct them to stop and ask the owner, not invent a link. Record the issue and amend the procedure; do not quietly coach the peer and call the handover independent.

Guided lab

  1. Write start-to-finish instructions and an explicit permitted-use statement.
  2. Add stop, failure, correction and shutdown procedures.
  3. Prepare a versioned sample input and expected output.
  4. Ask a peer to operate from the guide while you observe silently.
  5. Record difficulties and revise the guide.
  6. Submit the studio portfolio and name its actual release state.

What to submit

  • Operating guide
  • Peer walkthrough log
  • Ownership and maintenance map
  • Studio portfolio

Offline and access-limited route

Use the supplied practice table and worked example rather than a live service. Complete the same reasoning, calculations, state traces or manual artifact. Ask a peer to challenge the result. Label the work manual/simulated and identify which live-tool, audio, integration, permissions or production behaviours have not been tested. Do not claim a simulated control was enforced by real software.

Professional transfer

Choose your own professional context and write one safe adaptation: an engineering document review, a public health-education task, a campaign brief, or an art/design workflow. Name the authorised reviewer, permitted inputs and one task outside scope. Use invented data unless you have explicit permission; do not make a second full project this week.

Assessment

Dimension Maximum points
Write usable instructions 4
Define stop/recovery paths 4
Demonstrate peer operation 4
State ownership and release status 4

Provisional pass: 12/16 with no unresolved safety-critical issue. Score each dimension 0 (missing/unsafe), 1 (major gaps), 2 (partial), 3 (sound with minor omissions), or 4 (clear and evidenced). A learner may pass by honestly diagnosing a failed workflow; this is not production authorisation.

Safety and required remediation

Do not share private account credentials with classmates. Keep ownership transfer separate from public documentation.

Correct any unauthorised data use, fabricated evidence, hidden failure, unapproved consequential action or false claim of production testing before passing. Keep the actual release state explicit.

Reflection

What changed after you checked the evidence? Which failure would matter most in your profession? What remains unassessed? Record one concrete improvement and one situation where you would stop.

Instructor answer guidance

Instructor companion and answer guidance

Keep this section for feedback after the independent attempt. The separate instructor/pilot guide includes delivery preparation and assessment calibration.

Expected worked result: During a peer walkthrough, the reviewer discovers the registration field is absent. The runbook should direct them to stop and ask the owner, not invent a link. Record the issue and amend the procedure; do not quietly coach the peer and call the handover independent.

1. Should passwords appear in a handover document?

No. Use approved credential-transfer processes.

2. Does a pilot handover prove production readiness?

No. State the actual controls and approval status.

3. What should you do when a peer needs coaching?

Record the dependency and improve the documentation before another attempt.

Facilitation: ask the learner to point to a source, calculation, state transition or artifact supporting their conclusion. Accept defensible alternatives with explicit assumptions. Do not reward confident wording without evidence.

Remediation: identify the failed criterion, explain its consequence, provide a new small input, and reassess the relevant behaviour. Record what was actually observed; do not issue professional approval from a course score.
