# Week 24 - Demonstrate, defend and hand over

**Status:** teaching draft, not learner-tested. Fictional data and worked examples are labelled throughout. No production service is deployed by completing this lesson.

**Time:** 6 hours (360 learning minutes), with breaks additional. **Prerequisites:** the earlier course phases; review unfamiliar terms with the instructor.

**Materials:** this guide, the supplied workbook and diagram, a document editor or paper, and optionally an approved AI assistant. Do not buy a subscription for a lab without checking the dated cohort access plan.

## Learning outcomes

- Demonstrate the actual release state accurately.
- Defend design decisions using evidence.
- Hand over operation, correction and maintenance.

## Start with a prediction

Before reading the worked solution, write what you expect to happen, what could be wrong, and what evidence you would need to trust the result. Keep the note for your final reflection.

## Learner explanations

### Show the actual state

A prototype, staging pilot and authorised production release are different achievements. Show what a real user can do and which controls have actually been tested. A static demo is not an operational SaaS service, and an instructional media plan is not a published campaign.

### Defend decisions, not hype

Explain the user need, baseline, approach, rejected alternative, major failure, revision and remaining risk. Show source evidence and the cost of human review. Avoid universal claims from a small capstone or implying professional accreditation.

### Handover is ongoing responsibility

Name who owns updates, costs, incident response, correction and shutdown. Real release requires the relevant approvals and seven days of appropriate observation; if the calendar or approvals do not permit this, record the state reached and complete the release designation later. Academic performance and production authorisation are separate.

## Glossary

| Term | Plain-language meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Production | An authorised real release to its intended audience with operating ownership. |
| Rollback | Returning to a known permitted version or safe stopped state. |
| Observation window | A defined post-release period for inspecting use and problems. |
| Maintenance | Ongoing updates, corrections, cost review and eventual shutdown. |

## Worked example

A strong defence can conclude: 'The prototype works on our 12 declared cases, with two initial failures documented and revised. It is not authorised for external use. The owner will decide after the remaining review.' That is more credible than claiming a production-ready platform without approvals or real use.

## Guided lab

1. Prepare the 12-minute evidence-led demonstration.
2. Show one important failure and one rejected approach.
3. Have a peer follow the operating guide without coaching.
4. Complete owner, cost, update, correction and shutdown records.
5. If authorised, release to the agreed audience and observe seven days; otherwise keep the labelled restricted state.
6. Submit the portfolio and separate academic assessment from release approval.

## What to submit

- Final artifact and presentation
- Evaluation/source/risk portfolio
- Operating and maintenance guide
- Owner acceptance and accurate release record

## Professional transfer

Choose your own professional context and write one safe adaptation: an engineering document review, a public health-education task, a campaign brief, or an art/design workflow. Name the authorised reviewer, permitted inputs and one task outside scope. Use invented data unless you have explicit permission; do not make a second full project this week.

## Assessment

| Dimension | Maximum points |
| --- | --- |
| Demonstrate actual behaviour | 4 |
| Defend evidence and limits | 4 |
| Complete usable handover | 4 |
| State release authority accurately | 4 |

Provisional pass: **12/16** with no unresolved safety-critical issue. Score each dimension 0 (missing/unsafe), 1 (major gaps), 2 (partial), 3 (sound with minor omissions), or 4 (clear and evidenced). A learner may pass by honestly diagnosing a failed workflow; this is not production authorisation.

## Safety and required remediation

No automatic certificates of professional authority. Publish only consented work and appropriately licensed assets with actual owner approval.

Correct any unauthorised data use, fabricated evidence, hidden failure, unapproved consequential action or false claim of production testing before passing. Keep the actual release state explicit.

## Instructor companion and answer guidance

Keep this section for feedback after the independent attempt. The separate instructor/pilot guide includes delivery preparation and assessment calibration.

**Expected worked result:** A strong defence can conclude: 'The prototype works on our 12 declared cases, with two initial failures documented and revised. It is not authorised for external use. The owner will decide after the remaining review.' That is more credible than claiming a production-ready platform without approvals or real use.

**1. Does a high course mark authorise clinical deployment?**

No. Professional, institutional and legal approvals are separate.

**2. What if seven observation days exceed the course calendar?**

Finish the production designation later; do not shorten or invent observation evidence.

**3. Who maintains the project after graduation?**

A named owner who has accepted responsibility, not automatically the instructor or model provider.

**Facilitation:** ask the learner to point to a source, calculation, state transition or artifact supporting their conclusion. Accept defensible alternatives with explicit assumptions. Do not reward confident wording without evidence.

**Remediation:** identify the failed criterion, explain its consequence, provide a new small input, and reassess the relevant behaviour. Record what was actually observed; do not issue professional approval from a course score.
