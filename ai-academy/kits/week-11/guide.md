# Week 11 - Images and visual design

**Status:** teaching draft, not learner-tested. Fictional data and worked examples are labelled throughout. No production service is deployed by completing this lesson.

**Time:** 6 hours (360 learning minutes), with breaks additional. **Prerequisites:** the earlier course phases; review unfamiliar terms with the instructor.

**Materials:** this guide, the supplied workbook and diagram, a document editor or paper, and optionally an approved AI assistant. Do not buy a subscription for a lab without checking the dated cohort access plan.

## Learning outcomes

- Distinguish visual generation from visual interpretation.
- Create a brief specifying composition, use and constraints.
- Check rights, consistency, accessibility and production suitability.

## Start with a prediction

Before reading the worked solution, write what you expect to happen, what could be wrong, and what evidence you would need to trust the result. Keep the note for your final reflection.

## Learner explanations

### Direct the work

Describe the audience, intended channel, message, aspect ratio, hierarchy and assets you may use. A long list of style adjectives is not a production brief. Keep important factual text in an editable design layer so it can be checked and corrected.

### Generation is not measurement

A visually convincing object can be physically impossible or unsuitable for manufacture. A model's description of an image can also be wrong. Check logos, text, anatomy, geometry and claims against the actual requirements and appropriate expertise.

### Rights and provenance

Record the origin and permitted use of reference images, generated assets, fonts and logos. Platform permission is not a guarantee that every output is free of third-party rights. Obtain consent for identifiable people and avoid deceptive impersonation. Keep an asset register and disclose material AI use where required.

## Glossary

| Term | Plain-language meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Composition | The arrangement and visual hierarchy of a design. |
| Aspect ratio | The relationship between an image's width and height. |
| Provenance | A record of where material came from and how it changed. |
| Usage rights | Permission for a specific use; not a guarantee of every third-party right. |

## Guided lab

1. Write a one-page creative brief with audience and measurable constraints.
2. Sketch three distinct compositions before using a generator.
3. Use a permitted image tool such as Higgsfield if available, recording settings and costs.
4. Alternatively create a manual concept board and critique the supplied candidates.
5. Select a direction against the brief, not the most impressive effect.
6. Deliver an editable composition, export, alternative text and rights register.

## What to submit

- Creative brief
- Three-direction concept board
- Selected visual or annotated manual mock-up
- Alt text and provenance log

## Assessment

| Dimension | Maximum points |
| --- | --- |
| Write concrete design constraints | 4 |
| Compare genuine alternatives | 4 |
| Check factual and rights issues | 4 |
| Prepare accessible production assets | 4 |

Provisional pass: **12/16** with no unresolved safety-critical issue. Score each dimension 0 (missing/unsafe), 1 (major gaps), 2 (partial), 3 (sound with minor omissions), or 4 (clear and evidenced). A learner may pass by honestly diagnosing a failed workflow; this is not production authorisation.

## Safety and required remediation

Do not use unconsented real-person likenesses, unauthorised brand assets or a fabricated certification mark. A supplied concept description is not an actual vendor-generated image.

Correct any unauthorised data use, fabricated evidence, hidden failure, unapproved consequential action or false claim of production testing before passing. Keep the actual release state explicit.

## Instructor companion and answer guidance

Keep this section for feedback after the independent attempt. The separate instructor/pilot guide includes delivery preparation and assessment calibration.

**Expected worked result:** For the fictional workshop, choose a geometric learning motif rather than a realistic photograph implying a confirmed venue. Keep the event details in editable text, preserve known facts and label unknown logistics. Reject B's badge even if the image is attractive. Candidate A still needs human design and rights checks.

**1. Does generation permission settle every rights issue?**

No. Check applicable terms, references, consent and third-party rights.

**2. Can a venue photograph be an unsupported claim?**

Yes, if it implies a real location that is not established.

**3. Why keep text editable?**

To verify facts, correct typography and adapt accessible versions.

**Facilitation:** ask the learner to point to a source, calculation, state transition or artifact supporting their conclusion. Accept defensible alternatives with explicit assumptions. Do not reward confident wording without evidence.

**Remediation:** identify the failed criterion, explain its consequence, provide a new small input, and reassess the relevant behaviour. Record what was actually observed; do not issue professional approval from a course score.
