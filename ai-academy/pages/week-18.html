WEEK 18 / TEACHING DRAFT

Build the first professional workflow

Make one complete, inspectable journey before adding features.

Download the complete kit ↓

Status: teaching draft, not learner-tested. Fictional data and worked examples are labelled throughout. No production service is deployed by completing this lesson.

Time: 6 hours (360 learning minutes), with breaks additional. Prerequisites: the earlier course phases; review unfamiliar terms with the instructor.

Materials: this guide, the supplied workbook and diagram, a document editor or paper, and optionally an approved AI assistant. Do not buy a subscription for a lab without checking the dated cohort access plan.

Learning outcomes

  • Translate a brief into an ordered workflow.
  • Version prompts, assets and configuration.
  • Demonstrate a first complete path with human checkpoints.

Six-hour plan

Start with a prediction

Before reading the worked solution, write what you expect to happen, what could be wrong, and what evidence you would need to trust the result. Keep the note for your final reflection.

Learner explanations

Complete before complicated

A vertical slice takes one safe example from permitted input through output and review. It can be manually orchestrated at first. Automation of a broken process only makes failure faster. Keep the project small enough to inspect every step.

Leave a trace

Record sources, prompt versions, asset identifiers, settings, dependencies and outputs. A reviewer should know which version they are judging. Do not put credentials in the record. Distinguish vendor-interface actions from code/API automation rather than describing all activity as an integration.

Professional review is part of the build

Engineering, healthcare, creative and content tasks have different standards, but each needs appropriate ownership. Use qualified review for safety-sensitive claims and approved rights for media. Core learners can build no-code workflows; independent coding remains optional and may require additional skills.

Glossary

Term Plain-language meaning
Vertical slice One narrow path that works through the relevant stages of a workflow.
Prototype An exploratory version, not proof of production readiness.
Version An identifiable state of a prompt, asset, data source or system.
Evidence register A record linking outputs and decisions to their supporting material.

Worked example

The first invitation workflow can be four documents and a checklist rather than a coded platform. If a reviewer changes the date after layout, the approval must refer to the revised version. A screenshot of an attractive page is not evidence that permissions, persistence or failure handling work.

Guided lab

  1. Break the approved charter into ordered steps.
  2. Prepare one permitted sample input and a version identifier.
  3. Build the smallest complete journey using the chosen project kit.
  4. Record each intermediate artifact and review event.
  5. Have a partner follow the instructions without your narration.
  6. List the missing behaviours before expanding the feature set.

What to submit

  • Working vertical slice
  • Versioned instructions and artifacts
  • Review records
  • Known limitations/backlog

Offline and access-limited route

Use the supplied practice table and worked example rather than a live service. Complete the same reasoning, calculations, state traces or manual artifact. Ask a peer to challenge the result. Label the work manual/simulated and identify which live-tool, audio, integration, permissions or production behaviours have not been tested. Do not claim a simulated control was enforced by real software.

Professional transfer

Choose your own professional context and write one safe adaptation: an engineering document review, a public health-education task, a campaign brief, or an art/design workflow. Name the authorised reviewer, permitted inputs and one task outside scope. Use invented data unless you have explicit permission; do not make a second full project this week.

Self-check

  1. Does manual orchestration make the prototype invalid?
  2. Can a screenshot prove access control?
  3. What happens when an approved artifact changes?

Assessment

Dimension Maximum points
Complete one journey 4
Preserve reproducible records 4
Place meaningful review 4
Identify missing behaviour 4

Provisional pass: 12/16 with no unresolved safety-critical issue. Score each dimension 0 (missing/unsafe), 1 (major gaps), 2 (partial), 3 (sound with minor omissions), or 4 (clear and evidenced). A learner may pass by honestly diagnosing a failed workflow; this is not production authorisation.

Safety and required remediation

Use test data and restricted access. Do not deploy an unfinished application or publish media simply to make the demonstration look complete.

Correct any unauthorised data use, fabricated evidence, hidden failure, unapproved consequential action or false claim of production testing before passing. Keep the actual release state explicit.

Reflection

What changed after you checked the evidence? Which failure would matter most in your profession? What remains unassessed? Record one concrete improvement and one situation where you would stop.

Instructor answer guidance

Instructor companion and answer guidance

Keep this section for feedback after the independent attempt. The separate instructor/pilot guide includes delivery preparation and assessment calibration.

Expected worked result: The first invitation workflow can be four documents and a checklist rather than a coded platform. If a reviewer changes the date after layout, the approval must refer to the revised version. A screenshot of an attractive page is not evidence that permissions, persistence or failure handling work.

1. Does manual orchestration make the prototype invalid?

No. Label it accurately and evaluate its usefulness.

2. Can a screenshot prove access control?

No. Test the actual implemented boundary.

3. What happens when an approved artifact changes?

Assess the change and obtain approval for the changed version where required.

Facilitation: ask the learner to point to a source, calculation, state transition or artifact supporting their conclusion. Accept defensible alternatives with explicit assumptions. Do not reward confident wording without evidence.

Remediation: identify the failed criterion, explain its consequence, provide a new small input, and reassess the relevant behaviour. Record what was actually observed; do not issue professional approval from a course score.
